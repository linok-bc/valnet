How it works · Samuel's joint heading and cross-track estimator

Inside Samuel's runway fit

Guess the aircraft's heading and sideways offset, draw the runway the camera would see from there, and compare it with the detected runway. Adjust both until the two match. Then smooth the answer over time with a filter that knows how an aircraft drifts sideways when it points off the runway.

Taken as known (from the aircraft's own sensors)

  • pitch and roll — attitude
  • height above the runway
  • along-track distance to the threshold
  • runway width and length — see the note at the bottom

Solved from the image (the two unknowns)

  • heading relative to the runway — degrees, + nose right
  • cross-track offset from the centreline — metres, + right on this page; his code stores + left

The pipeline, one camera frame at a time

  1. 1

    Detect the runway

    YOLOv8 segmentation gives a soft mask: each pixel's runway probability, times the detection's confidence.

  2. 2

    Crop and shrink

    Take a box around the mask with a 48 px margin, enlarged to also cover where the filter expects the runway. Resample that box to a fixed 48 × 48 grid, whatever its size in the image. Everything after this compares 48 × 48 = 2,304 numbers.

    roi_margin_px = 48downsample_size = 48
  3. 3

    Render a candidate runway

    For a guessed heading and cross-track, plus the known inputs, project the runway's four corners into the camera. Then compute how much of each 48 × 48 cell the runway covers, with slightly soft edges. This "analytic" renderer is smooth, so a tiny change in the guess gives a tiny change in the picture, which is what lets the solver take precise steps.

    renderer = "analytic"edge blur σ = 1.5 px
  4. 4

    Score the match

    Cost = mean squared difference between the rendered patch and the observed patch. Zero would be a perfect overlap.

    metric = L2
  5. 5

    Search for the lowest cost

    First frame, or after losing track: a coarse-to-fine grid search. Try every combination over ±18° and ±140 m in steps of 2° and 10 m, keep the best 3, and zoom in twice (0.5°/2.5 m, then 0.125°/0.625 m). Every other frame: skip the grid and run Levenberg–Marquardt (a Gauss–Newton solver with damping) straight from the filter's prediction, for up to 30 steps.

    cold: 3 levels, beam 3warm: LM only, ≤ 30 iterations
  6. 6

    Filter over time

    A Kalman filter tracks heading, heading rate and cross-track. Between frames it predicts the sideways drift from geometry: distance flown × tan(heading), with the distance known from the along-track input. It then checks the new fit against that prediction. A fit that disagrees too much is rejected (NIS above 13.8, the 99.9% χ² gate), and 3 rejections in a row trigger a fresh grid search. Otherwise it blends the fit in, treating every fit as equally noisy: 0.15° and 2 m.

    state = [heading, heading rate, cross-track]R = (0.15°)², (2 m)²gate χ²₂ 99.9 % = 13.8
The loop that makes it fast. The filter's prediction for the next frame becomes both the solver's starting guess (step 5) and part of the crop box (step 2). A good track keeps each frame to a few solver steps (about 10–30 ms), instead of a grid search over thousands of candidates.

The dark band on the cost map runs diagonally: a little more heading and a little less cross-track produce nearly the same picture. On these grids the two are strongly anti-correlated along the low-cost band (r = −0.83 far, −0.88 near). The filter's job is largely to break that tie, using the fact that heading makes cross-track change over the following frames.

Where this points for improvements

  • Step 6 treats every fit alike. The solver already computes each fit's own uncertainty, but the filter ignores it and uses fixed noise values. Using the fit's uncertainty, or a range-dependent value, would let close-range fits through undamped.
  • Step 2 squeezes the runway into 48 × 48. At 400 m each patch cell averages about 4 × 4 camera pixels, so edge detail is lost exactly where it is richest. A larger grid close in (his ablation suite already has a 96-pixel option) is a cheap test.
  • The runway size is fitted from the labels. Width, length and threshold position come from a per-flight fit to the ground-truth corners, not from a runway database. The fitted widths run 1–2% narrower than the database's. A fair comparison should use the database values.
