Which camera method should estimate cross-track on final approach?

Michael Lin and Samuel · 13 held-out X-Plane 12 approaches · truth from the rotation-fixed logs

We compared four ways of reading the aircraft's lateral offset from the runway centreline out of a single forward camera. Fusing Samuel's heading-and-offset filter with the edge-line tilt measurement is the most accurate inside 1 km. On their own, the two methods are statistically tied there.

Bottom line

  1. Fused (Samuel's filter + tilt) is best inside 1 km: mean error, against for Samuel alone and for tilt alone. It clearly beats tilt; against Samuel the gain is likely but not yet proven on 13 flights.
  2. Samuel and tilt alone are a statistical tie inside 1 km. They fail on different flights, which is why fusing them helps.
  3. Samuel is best beyond 1 km and is the only method that also gives heading, at mean error from 1.5 km.
  4. Bird's-eye view is out: about 2 m off even when handed the true heading.

The four methods

All four see the same camera frames. Three use the same stock YOLOv8 runway mask; BEV uses its own detector trained on warped images. Each is also given some flight data (the “known” inputs below), as on a real aircraft.

MethodHow it reads cross-trackAlso estimatesKnown inputs it needs
SamuelRenders the runway at a candidate heading and offset, adjusts both until it matches the mask; a kinematic filter tracks them over timeHeadingpitch, roll, height, along-track distance, runway width
Edge-line tiltHow much the two runway edges lean in the image. Leaning depends on lateral offset but not on heading—pitch, roll, runway width
Bird's-eye viewWarps the image to a top-down view and reads the runway's front edge (Ryu et al., 2024), then a Kalman filter—pitch, roll, height, runway width, true heading from the INS
FusedSamuel's filter, with tilt added as a second cross-track measurement each frame, weighted by how noisy tilt is at that rangeHeadingsame as Samuel

All four methods, on the two flights no model has seen

BEV's detector was trained on a different split and saw most of the 13 test flights in training. Only flights 145 and 146 (Gällivare, both runway directions) are unseen by every model, so the four-way comparison is limited to those two.

BEV is consistently about 2 m right of the truth on both flights, even though it is given the true heading from the INS. With only two flights, treat this table as a demonstration, not a ranking. The 13-flight results above are the ones to rely on for the other three.

How this was scored

  • Data. X-Plane 12 approaches, 3° glideslope, captured from about 3 km to 150 m. Beyond 1.5 km the aircraft is still turning onto the localizer, so the experiment starts at 1.5 km. Heading is scored from 1.5 km and cross-track from 1 km.
  • Held out. The 13 test flights were never used to train the runway detector shared by Samuel's method, tilt and fused. The tilt noise model used by the fusion was fitted on 10 separate validation flights, so nothing was tuned on the test flights.
  • Truth. The logged position and heading, after this week's fix that removed a per-flight rotation of up to about 0.5° from the logs. Samuel's per-flight runway geometry was refitted on the fixed logs. A constant offset of about 0.25 m between the logs and the runway as it appears in the images remains, so errors near 0.25 m are partly that floor.
  • Filtering. Samuel's and fused use Samuel's kinematic filter. Tilt uses a simple Kalman filter, and BEV uses the paper's. Unfiltered, tilt averages , so the filter is not what separates the methods.
  • Not yet tested. Noise in the known inputs (pitch, roll, height, along-track distance, runway width). That is where tilt, which needs the fewest inputs, should have an advantage.

Next steps

  1. Fuse tilt only where it helps. Choose the cut-off range on the validation flights, which should recover Samuel's accuracy beyond 1 km while keeping the gain inside it.
  2. Input-noise robustness. Perturb pitch, roll, height, along-track distance and runway width for all three methods. This decides whether needing fewer inputs matters in practice.
  3. More held-out flights. Retrain BEV on the shared split so all four methods can be compared on all 13 flights, and firm up the fused-vs-Samuel result.
  4. Real footage. Run the fused method on real approach videos to check how it transfers beyond the simulator.
